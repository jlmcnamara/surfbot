The rule

A date earns the ✓ Verified badge only when it can be matched, character-for-character, against a document the company itself published. We run an automated re-verification pass that fetches the source and confirms the date is still there.

The verification pipeline

  1. Resolve the company. Map the ticker to its SEC CIK using the SEC's official company_tickers.json.
  2. Pull primary filings. Read the company's recent Form 8-K filings from the SEC's data.sec.gov submissions API — these are filed by the company, under penalty of law.
  3. Match the date. Scan the filing (and the company's IR/press page) for an earnings-announcement sentence and extract the date next to it.
  4. Record provenance. Store the exact quote, the source URL, and the retrieval timestamp. You can read all of it on each company page.
  5. Re-verify. python verifier.py reverify re-fetches every source and flags anything that no longer matches.

What "pending" means

Many companies have not yet announced their next earnings date. Third-party sites fill that gap with an estimate — a guess based on last year's pattern. We don't. A pending company shows no date at all until it publishes one. That is the entire point.
